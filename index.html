    How to use MotionLab

    1. Film. Set the camera side-on to the movement, level and still (a tripod helps), and as far away as practical: zoom in rather than standing close. Keep the whole movement in frame. Put a reference of known length, such as a 1 m stick, in the same plane as the movement, standing on the ground. For fast movements, film in slow motion with your phone's Camera app, then use Import video.
    2. Calibrate. Tap the ruler button, tap the bottom of the reference, then the top, and enter its length in metres.
    3. Auto-track (optional). Tap the wand button. Detect places the head, shoulder, elbow, wrist, hip, knee, ankle and toe. Follow tracks one marker, such as a ball, after you place it by hand on one frame. Automatically placed points have a white outline. Check them and tap to correct any that jump.
    4. Digitise by hand. Choose a marker below the video and tap where it is. With Next marker on, the app moves through the markers and then to the next frame. Pinch or use the mouse wheel to zoom, drag to move around. On a keyboard, ← and → step through frames.
    5. Results. The graph button shows angle–time graphs, projectile motion and a CSV export for Excel or Google Sheets.

    Notes

    • All values are 2D projections onto the camera's view. Movement towards or away from the camera causes error.
    • Joint angles are included angles (180° = straight limb). Flexion = 180° − included angle for the knee, hip and elbow.
    • For projectile values, choose frames where the object is fully in the air. Gravity from data should be close to 9.81 m/s² when digitising and calibration are good.
    • For slow-motion clips, check Capture frame rate in Trial settings (the gear button) matches the rate it was filmed at.

    Privacy

    Videos and results stay in this browser on this device. Nothing is uploaded. Clearing the browser's website data deletes them, so export the CSV of anything you want to keep. Automatic tracking uses Google's MediaPipe, which runs on the device but sends anonymous usage statistics (not images) to Google.
